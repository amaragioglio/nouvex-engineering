Imaging and microstructure.

Use the appropriate imaging scale to document morphology, interfaces, defects, and internal structure.

  • SEM imaging supports examination of surface morphology, fracture features, particles, coatings, and process-related defects.
  • TEM imaging supports thin-section analysis of internal structure, interfaces, and nanoscale features.
  • Keyence digital microscopy experience supports optical inspection, image documentation, and compatible 2D or 3D measurement workflows.

Site-specific sectioning and preparation.

Focused ion beam (FIB) methods can expose a precise cross-section, isolate a region of interest, or prepare a thin lamella for subsequent TEM work.

Because ion-beam milling is localized and generally destructive, the target region, preparation sequence, and risk to beam-sensitive materials are reviewed during scoping.

Surface and vacuum chemistry.

Surface-sensitive and vacuum methods help separate a material problem from residue, contamination, outgassing, or process-environment effects.

  • Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) supports surface-sensitive elemental, isotopic, and molecular information, including chemical imaging and depth-dependent workflows when appropriate.
  • Residual gas analysis (RGA) supports vacuum contamination, leak, outgassing, and process investigations by examining gas species present in a vacuum environment.

Electrical and photovoltaic behavior.

Connect material condition and device structure to measured electrical performance.

  • Four-point probe analysis supports sheet-resistance, resistivity, and uniformity questions for compatible films, substrates, wafers, and semiconductor samples.
  • Photovoltaic testing is scoped around the cell or module type, performance question, test condition, metric, and decision the result must support.

Molecular, elemental, and optical characterization.

Use complementary measurements to examine molecular structure, trace elements, and interaction with ultraviolet or visible light.

  • FTIR spectroscopy supports molecular-structure questions, material identification, and comparison of changes in functional groups.
  • ICP-MS supports multi-element and trace-element measurements in compatible liquids, extracts, or digested samples.
  • UV-Vis spectroscopy supports absorbance, transmittance, reflectance, and appropriate optical-property measurements in the ultraviolet and visible range.

Start with the sample and the decision.

A concise description of the material and the reason for the analysis is more useful than selecting an instrument without context.

  • Material, coating, device, residue, liquid, film, wafer, component, or sample type
  • Observed defect, contamination, variation, degradation, or performance question
  • Feature size, depth, concentration, or electrical range when known
  • Sample dimensions, quantity, handling limits, and whether destructive preparation is acceptable
  • Reference, control, or comparison samples
  • Decision the result must support and the required deliverable

Methods and deliverables are confirmed before work begins.

Instrument configuration, sample preparation, measurement range, detection or resolution limits, destructive steps, quality controls, turnaround, and report format depend on the project. Nouvex confirms the analysis path during scoping rather than implying that every method fits every sample.
