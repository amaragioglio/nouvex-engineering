02 / Materials analysis

Materials analysis for technical decisions.

Frame the observation, comparison, contamination, surface, or failure question before choosing the method and deliverable.

Analysis paths

Start with evidence, not an acronym.

The available technique and scope are confirmed after reviewing the sample, question, preparation needs, and expected result.

  • Microscopy and visual characterization
  • X-ray analysis methods
  • Surface analysis and characterization
  • Sample preparation
  • Technical interpretation and next-step planning

Useful context for the first review.

A concise description of the material and the decision behind the analysis is more useful than selecting an instrument without context.

  • Material, coating, residue, component, or sample type
  • Known history, processing conditions, and comparison samples
  • Observed defect, contamination, wear, fracture, or variation
  • Feature size or depth scale when known
  • Decision the result must support and required timeline

Scope is confirmed before work begins.

Specific instruments, test methods, sample limits, detection limits, destructive preparation, turnaround, and report format depend on the project. Nouvex confirms these details during scoping rather than implying that every technique fits every sample.
